Most of a backlink profile doesn't count.

Backlink tools count links. Many of the links they count were bought in bulk or are plain spam, and Google most likely ignores them. Count them anyway and your competitors look further ahead than they are, and the budget you ask for is too big.

Four steps: check, clean, compare, plan.

One question per screen, answered with numbers you can check.

  1. 1

    Check

    Enter your domain. linkgap fetches your backlinks and suggests competitors that rank for the same searches. You don't need a data account; checks run on linkgap's, out of your free allowance. The result is one card: the gap, your good linking sites, how many competitors were analysed, and a warning when a link network is involved. Sample projects are badged as samples, so made-up numbers never mix with real ones.

  2. 2

    Clean

    Every linking site is rated good, weak or spam by rules you can see: spam score, very low authority, how many sites it links out to, spammy anchor texts, broken links. Hover a reason and it shows how many points it added and where the threshold is. Disagree? Change the verdict for one site or many at once, and undo it if you change your mind.

  3. 3

    Compare

    With the spam and networks out, the comparison means something: how many good linking sites you're behind the typical competitor, the top three or the leader. Then the other half of the answer: every good site that links to at least two competitors and not to you. Here that was 2,221 sources, 84 of them strong enough to go after first. Export them as CSV.

  4. 4

    Plan

    Set a budget. linkgap picks from real marketplace offers, cheapest first or best value per euro, skips sites that already link to you, and shows surcharges and writing fees as their own columns, so the total is what you'd actually pay. In this case €1,500 bought 11 links for €1,388.58. More on what that buys you below.

Link networks

The 364-site network three competitors were sharing.

A link network is a group of sites that exist to pass links, usually sold as a package. Here, 364 unrelated sites linked to the same three competitors and to nobody else we measured. They also shared the same authority scores over and over: 83 sites all had authority 51, 50 had 23, 25 had 79. Real sites rarely share exact scores like that. linkgap flags the group, shows the evidence, and stops counting it.

Why it was flagged

Exclusive footprint
Every one of the 364 sites links to at least 2 of these 3 competitors, and to no other analysed site.
Repeated scores
The same authority values again and again: 51 ×83, 23 ×50, 79 ×25.
Very low authority
Median authority 2 out of 100.
Unrelated topics
Only 46 of 364 site names share even a word with the competitors' own names.
Brand or empty anchors
65% of the links carry no anchor text at all, or just the name of the site they point to.

What a budget buys you.

For a new site, the first links matter most, and each one after that adds a little less. linkgap weighs links by authority instead of counting them, so a strong bought link can stand in for several of the weak ones behind a competitor's number. The effect figures are a model, not a ranking forecast.

  • 10 links carry 63% of the estimated effect, for about €1,100 (model, not a ranking forecast)
  • 11 links for €1,388.58 on a €1,500 budget: 63.6% of the way to the competitor's link strength (model)
  • about 80 paid links, about €10,500, to match their link strength in full, not 217

Words you'll see.

Plain meanings, and the label each one has in the app.

Linking sites in the app: referring domains
Different websites that link to a site. Ten links from one website count as one linking site.
Authority and DR in the app: Authority, DR
Authority is a 0–100 strength score for a website, based on DataForSEO's data. DR is the similar score link portals advertise on their offers. They're related, not identical.
Typical competitor in the app: median
The competitor in the middle when you line them all up. One giant or one tiny competitor doesn't skew it.
Projected in the app: sampled
For very large profiles only part is fetched, and the totals are estimated from it. linkgap always labels these.
Link strength in the app: link strength, link value
Links weighted by authority instead of simply counted, so one strong site outweighs several weak ones.
Estimated effect in the app: Estimated effect (model)
How far a set of links gets you towards a competitor's link strength, on a curve where the first links count most. A model, not a ranking forecast.

Questions

Isn't buying links against Google's rules?

Google's spam policies name buying links for ranking purposes as link spam. linkgap takes bought networks out of the count, sells no links, makes no ranking promise, and only plans with offers whose DR is backed by traffic or price. The list of link sources is just as useful for earning links. Whether and how you build links is your decision.

Ahrefs and Semrush already have a gap tool. Why this?

They list the links. linkgap shows which of them count, and what the ones that do would cost. Use both.

What data sources does it use?
